What a 4AM Shift Actually Looks Like

By Marcus on February 18, 2026 in Behind the Scenes

Home/Blog/What a 4AM Shift Actually Looks Like

Long before the doors open, the ovens are already going. Most customers see the bakery at its calmest — warm light, full shelves, the smell of fresh bread. What they don't see is the three hours before that, when it's just two of us, a dark kitchen, and a very long prep list.

My alarm goes off at 3:40AM. By 4:15 I'm unlocking the back door, and by 4:20 the first tray of yesterday's shaped dough is coming out of the walk-in fridge, ready to bake.

"By the time customers walk in, we've already been awake longer than most people's whole workday."

4:20AM — First bake

The sourdough that was shaped and retarded overnight goes in first, since it needs the longest bake and the most attention to timing. While that's going, we start mixing the day's bagel dough and setting the pot of water to boil.

Shaping is the part that takes the longest to learn — not the strength, but the touch. Too rough and you knock all the air out; too gentle and the loaf won't hold its shape through the final proof.

6:00AM — Doors unlocked

By the time the first customer walks in, we've usually got three ovens' worth of bread cooling, the pastry case half-filled, and coffee that's gone cold twice already. It's the best part of the shift — watching someone's face when they smell the bread for the first time.
