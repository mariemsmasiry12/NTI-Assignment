Where Our Day-Old Bread Actually Goes

By Marcus on January 9, 2026 in Community

Home/Blog/Where Our Day-Old Bread Actually Goes

Nothing we bake goes to waste. It's a small line to put in a blog post, but it's been a fixed part of how this bakery runs since the day it opened, long before "food waste" was something anyone talked about.

Every evening, whatever hasn't sold — day-old loaves, extra bagels, the pastries that didn't quite make the cut for the display case — gets boxed up and driven three blocks over to a shelter we've worked with for over a decade.

"Bread that doesn't get eaten isn't 'leftover' — it's just bread that hasn't found its table yet."

How the partnership started

It began almost by accident. One of our early bakers used to volunteer at the shelter on weekends and started bringing whatever was left at closing. What was an informal habit for a few months turned into a standing arrangement, and it's never really stopped since.

Most nights it's between fifteen and thirty loaves, plus whatever pastry is left. Not enough to feed everyone who needs it, but enough that it's genuinely part of someone's dinner most days of the week.

We don't do this for recognition — most customers have no idea it happens. But it felt worth writing down once, because it's the kind of thing a small, family-run bakery can actually do that a bigger operation often can't be bothered with.
