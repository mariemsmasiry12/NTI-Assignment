Boil Then Bake: Bagels at Home

By Layla on January 27, 2026 in Recipes

Home/Blog/Boil Then Bake: Bagels at Home

The lye bath is intimidating but not necessary. Commercial bakeries use food-grade lye because it browns the crust faster and gives that deep mahogany shine, but for a home kitchen a simple honey-water boil gets you about 90% of the way to a proper New York-style chew — without the safety gear.

The boil is the step people skip most often, and it's the one that actually makes a bagel a bagel rather than just a ring-shaped bread roll. It gelatinises the starch on the surface of the dough, which is what gives you that dense, chewy skin instead of a soft, bready crust.

"Skip the boil and you've made a very confused donut, not a bagel."

Our boil, step by step

Bring a wide, shallow pot of water to a rolling boil and stir in a tablespoon of honey and a teaspoon of baking soda per litre. Drop the shaped bagels in for 60 seconds a side — no longer, or the crumb turns gummy. Straight from the water onto a heavily floured peel, topped while still wet so the seeds actually stick.

Into a screaming hot oven straight after topping — 240°C (465°F), no fan, for around 18 minutes until deeply golden. Any hotter and the outside sets before the inside catches up.

The result won't be identical to a bakery lye-boiled bagel, but it'll be close enough that most people can't tell the difference — and it won't require a trip to a chemical supplier to make.
